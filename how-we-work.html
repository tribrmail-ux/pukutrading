ProcessEnquiry to delivery

You describe the problem. We come back with a price and a plan.

Five steps, in writing at every point where money is involved.

Nothing here is unusual. It is written down because plenty of buying goes wrong in the gap between what somebody asked for and what turned up on the truck, and that gap closes with detail.

From enquiry to delivery

  1. You send what you need

    A part number, a product data sheet, a photograph of the old drum, a measurement scrawled on the back of a delivery note, or a description of the job. Any of these is enough to start. The quickest route is WhatsApp; the form on this site does the same thing and lets you attach a file.

  2. We come back with questions — then we source

    Usually a few: what you are dosing into, what can offload at your site, whether an equivalent product is acceptable. Then we go to the market. We hold no agency and no allegiance, so we can ask several sources for the same item and take the one that suits your job.

  3. You get a written quotation

    Stating the product, the grade or profile, the packaging, the quantity, and what is included in getting it to you. If we are proposing a substitute for what you asked for, it is named as a substitute with the difference explained. If we cannot source something sensibly, we say that instead of quoting a number we do not believe.

  4. You approve, we place and follow the order

    Once you approve, the order is ours to manage: placing it, chasing it, arranging transport, handling the documentation, and telling you when something moves or slips. You deal with one person about it, not a chain of suppliers.

  5. It arrives, and we close the loop

    Delivered to your site with the paperwork — invoice, delivery note, and the safety data sheet where it applies. Check it before the truck leaves. If something is wrong, tell us and it becomes our problem to resolve with the supplier, which is a large part of what an agent is for.

The money

Where our margin comes from, plainly

We are paid out of the difference between what we buy at and what we sell at, and that is stated in one number on your quotation. No consulting fee, no charge for a quotation, no charge for the chlorine verification.

  • You are not paying for stockA distributor's price has to carry inventory: capital tied up, storage, shrinkage, and product that expired unsold. We buy against your order, so none of that sits on your invoice.
  • You are not paying for a branch networkNo trade counter, no fleet, no depot. The cost of running Puku is small and it stays out of your price.
  • Sourcing is competitive per orderBecause we are not tied to one manufacturer, each enquiry can be put to whoever is best placed for it that week.
  • What we do not doWe do not mark up freight invisibly, and we do not quote a delivered price and then add costs at the gate. Ask what is in the number and we will tell you.

Timing

How long things take

It depends on where the goods are, what transport is available and whether the item is standard or made to order. That is a real answer, not a dodge — anyone quoting you a fixed number of days for anything, sight unseen, is guessing.

What we will do is give you a realistic expectation with the quotation, and tell you when it changes. If you have a shutdown date, a planting window or a contractor booked, say so in the enquiry. It changes how we source, and sometimes what we recommend.
