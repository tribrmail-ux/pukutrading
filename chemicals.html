Chlorine strength verification

Hypochlorite is sold by strength and used by strength. It is very rarely checked.

The problem

Sodium hypochlorite starts breaking down as soon as it is made. Heat accelerates it, sunlight accelerates it, and so does time in a hot vehicle on a long road. A load that left the factory at 12.5 % available chlorine does not necessarily arrive at 12.5 %, and there is nothing on the outside of a drum that tells you what is inside it.

Two things follow. You may be paying for water. And your dosing may be set for a strength you no longer have — which is the more serious of the two, because it shows up as a residual you cannot hold rather than as a number on an invoice.

The test

We come to your site with the reagents and the glassware and run an iodometric titration on a sample drawn from your own stock, in front of you. Potassium iodide and acid release iodine in proportion to the available chlorine present; sodium thiosulphate is titrated in until the colour goes; starch indicator sharpens the endpoint. It is the standard method for available chlorine and it takes minutes, not days.

You get the available chlorine result in writing, against the strength you were invoiced for, with the date and the batch or delivery reference. That sheet is yours — use it with your current supplier if you want to. We will run the test whether or not you ever buy a litre from us.

Why we offer it

Because it is the fastest way to show what kind of supplier we intend to be, and because it is genuinely useful to the person running the plant. If your current chlorine is exactly what it should be, that is a good thing to know and we will tell you so. If it is not, you have a number, and a number is something you can act on.

Field titration on your samples. Not a laboratory certificate, and we do not claim it as one.
